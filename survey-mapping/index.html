SURVEY AND CARTOGRAPHY

MAPPING PROJECTS

Classic Maya Residences | Alacranes Bajo/Blue Creek, Northwestern Belize (2008-2014)

Basemaps:
🟩 Detail: Stamen Terrain
⚪ Overview: Esri Gray Canvas

In addition to serving as an archaeological crew chief on the excavation of several Classic Maya elite residences in northwestern Belize, I was also responsible for overseeing the total station mapping of approximately 20 sites. Using a Topcon GTS-2R EDM theodolite, I mapped architecture, excavation units, key landscape features, and generated topographic maps I trained both professional archaeologists and students in total station operation and field drafting techniques for plan and profile recording. I also managed all spatial data using Esri ArcGIS and ArcMap, ensuring accurate integration with the broader site maps and research goals. I assisted Bob Warden of Texas A&M University in LiDAR-based mapping to support topographic and architectural analysis.

Pueblo III-IV Pit House Village | El Morro, NM (2007)

While serving as crew chief for SWCA during the excavation of a late prehistoric Pueblo III–IV village near El Morro National Monument in New Mexico, I was responsible for total station mapping using a Nikon DTM 332 EDM. The site included three pit structures and approximately 50-75 features. I provided instruction in total station operation to professional archaeologists and oversaw all spatial data management in Esri ArcGIS and ArcMap, ensuring the accuracy and consistency of field records and site maps throughout the project.

Multiple Ancestral Pueblo Sites near Albuquerque, NM (2007)

During the Mid-America Pipeline Loop (MAPL) data recovery project conducted by the Office of Contract Archeology (OCA), I led total station mapping efforts at two significant sites: a late Pueblo agricultural settlement on Albuquerque’s West Mesa and an expansive Basketmaker III - Pueblo I hamlet on the Santa Ana Reservation. Mapping was performed using a Topcon GTS-226 EDM equipped with SurveyPro 2.0 software to ensure precise spatial documentation.

Terminal Solutrean/Early Magdalenian Cave Deposits | Northern Spain (2006)

As instrument man on the El Mirón Archaeological Project—directed by Dr. Lawrence Straus (University of New Mexico) and Dr. Manuel González Morales (University of Cantabria)—I was responsible for total station mapping in a cave site in Ramales de la Victoria, northern Spain. Using both a Leica TCRM 1105 and a Leica TCR 407 EDM, I mapped approximately 2,000 in situ artifacts located at the terminal Solutrean and initial Magdalenian interface, contributing to the high-resolution spatial documentation of Upper Paleolithic occupation levels.

Pueblo Bonito | Chaco Canyon, NM (2005)

Leading mapping efforts for the University of New Mexico’s Chaco Stratigraphy Program, I created a detailed topographic map of the mounds surrounding Pueblo Bonito and carried out a supplemental mapping initiative at Pueblo del Arroyo. I also established the excavation grid north of the Pueblo Bonito mounds during the program’s inaugural field season. Responsibilities included training both professional archaeologists and graduate students in the use of a Topcon GTS-226 EDM with SurveyPro 2.0 software and managing all spatial data using QGIS.

Old Alameda Plaza | Albuquerque, NM (2003-2004)

During the Alameda Area D Data Recovery Project in northwest Albuquerque, New Mexico, I directed total station mapping for the Office of Contract Archeology (OCA). My responsibilities included mapping over 100 archaeological features and approximately 90 human burials associated with the historic occupation of the Alameda Plaza (1710–1903), a very large site spanning over 500 meters in length. I also mapped Alameda Pueblo, an expansive Pueblo IV village under the modern-day elementary school in Alameda, NM. I managed all locational data using QGIS and provided field-based training in total station operation to 5–10 graduate students, using a Topcon GTS-2R EDM with SurveyPro 2.0 software.

Middle Archaic - El Paso Phase Jornada Mogollon Village | Alamogordo, NM (2004)

As crew chief for the Office of Contract Archeology (OCA) on the Alamogordo Flood Control Project, I conducted total station mapping of an extensive Middle Archaic–El Paso phase Mogollon village in Alamogordo, New Mexico. Mapping was performed using a Topcon GTS-2R EDM with SurveyPro 2.0 software. In addition to overseeing all spatial data collection, I provided training to several professional archaeologists in the operation of the total station.

Pueblo IV Agricultural Sites | Santa Fe, NM (2003-2004)

Over the course of two years, I conducted a series of arcaheological mapping projects for the Office of Contract Archeology (OCA) across central and northern New Mexico. These included the MAPL data recovery and auger testing programs, where I supervised total station mapping at 18 distinct archaeological sites using a Topcon GTS-2R EDM with SurveyPro 2.0 software. I also directed mapping efforts for the continuation of the Camel Tracks data recovery project, overseeing spatial documentation of ten Pueblo IV field house sites outside Santa Fe. In earlier phases of the Camel Tracks project, I served as instrument man, assisting in the mapping of ten additional sites in the La Cienega area. Across these assignments, I was responsible for all locational data and regularly trained students and professionals in the operation of the total station system, and managed all field data in QGIS.

Maness and Associates | Cortez, CO (2003)

While working as instrument man for Maness and Associates in Cortez, Colorado, I operated a total station on a range of professional land surveying projects under the mentorship of Ernest Maness, PLS. My work included conducting land title surveys, right-of-way stakeouts, and other cadastral mapping tasks across Montezuma County. This experience provided a valuable foundation in professional surveying methods and deepened my technical understanding of total station workflows beyond archaeological contexts.

Pueblo I Villages | Ridges Basin and Blue Mesa, Durango, CO (2002)

As crew chief for SWCA on the Animas–La Plata (A–LP) Archaeological Project near Durango, Colorado, I oversaw total station mapping of eight Pueblo I sites in Ridges Basin—mostly in the Eastern Cluster— and additional pit house villages in Blue Mesa. These excavations were conducted in advance of the construction of Lake Nighthorse. My responsibilities included supervising all mapping operations using a Nikon/TDS total station and providing instruction in total station use to Native American participants from the Ute Mountain Ute and Navajo tribes.

Archaic and Ancestral Pueblo Sites | NW New Mexico/SW Colorado (1999)

During the MAPL natural gas pipeline expansion project across northwest New Mexico and southwest Colorado, I served as Instrument Man for Woods Canyon Archaeological Consultants. I was responsible for mapping five archaeological sites ranging in age from Middle Archaic-Pueblo II using a Nikon/TDS total station, contributing high-precision spatial data to the project’s mitigation and documentation efforts.

CAJE Engineering | Montezuma County, CO (1998)

While working for CAJE Engineering across southwest Colorado, I served as instrument man under Party Chief Rich Feit. My duties included running a Sokkia/TDS total station, performing slope staking, and completing bench loops. One of the principal assignments involved establishing a traffic intersection at County Road G in Cortez.

Pueblo I and Gobernador Phase Navajo Sites | Navajo Reservoir, NM (1997-1998)

During my time with La Plata Archaeological Consultants, I worked as instrument man on the Alison CO2 pipeline expansion project. I was responsible for total station mapping of nine archaeological sites—ranging from Pueblo I occupations to Gobernador Phase Navajo components— impacted by pipeline construction in the Navajo Reservoir area of northern New Mexico.

Pueblo II-III Villages | Cowboy Wash, CO (1995)

While working on the Ute Mountain Ute Irrigated Lands Archaeological Project in Cowboy Wash, Colorado (near Towaoc), I served as instrument man for Soil Systems Inc. My responsibilities included overseeing total station mapping of seven large Pueblo II-III villages, contributing to a broader cultural resource management initiative for the Ute Mountain Ute Tribe.

Late Prehistoric Buffalo Jump | Gillette, WY (1991)

While working with Larson/Tibesar and Associates, I was responsible for total station mapping at two significant sites: a Late Prehistoric bison jump near Gillette, Wyoming, and portions of Fort Abraham Lincoln in Mandan, North Dakota. These projects supported archaeological documentation and heritage management efforts across the northern Plains.
