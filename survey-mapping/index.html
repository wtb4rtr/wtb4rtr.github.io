Mapping Projects

I have conducted 17 total station mapping projects of archaeological sites and communities, as well as two land surveying jobs. Below, you’ll find interactive maps and brief project descriptions that document my field mapping work across the U.S., Mexico, Belize, and Europe. These projects span cultural resource management, academic research, and professional land surveying.

EARLY TO LATE CLASSIC MAYA COURTYARDS | NOJOL NAH/BLUE CREEK, BELIZE (2008 - 2014)

Basemaps:
🟩 Close Up: Thunderforest Outdoors
⚪ Overview: Esri Gray Canvas

Nojol Nah is a large Classic Maya community composed of multiple elite residential courtyards in the Blue Creek region of northwestern Belize. I excavated at eight loci between 2008 and 2014, and I was responsible for overseeing the total station mapping of 24 courtyards at Nojol Nah and nearby communities. Using a Topcon GTS-2R EDM theodolite, I mapped architecture, excavation units, key landscape features, and generated topographic maps. I trained both professional archaeologists and students in total station operation and field drafting techniques for plan and profile recording. I also managed all spatial data using Esri ArcGIS and ArcMap to ensure accurate integration with broader site maps and research goals. In addition to my mapping responsibilities, I assisted Bob Warden of the Texas A&M University School of Architecture in 3D laser scanning and LiDAR documentation at 11 courtyards.

PUEBLO III-IV PIT HOUSE VILLAGE | EL MORRO VALLEY, NEW MEXICO (2007)

Basemaps:
🟩 Close Up: Thunderforest Outdoors
⚪ Overview: Esri Gray Canvas

Between March and August of 2007, I was the crew chief in charge of excavation and mapping on SWCA Environmental Consultants’ data recovery at Hokona Pueblo (LA 153714), located roughly three miles east of El Morro National Monument in the El Morro Valley of Cibola County, New Mexico. The site, positioned on the northeast-facing slope of a low basalt rise, was determined to be a multi-component Pueblo III–IV habitation, occupied between A.D. 1275 and 1350. Excavations revealed 45 cultural features, including three pit structures, a masonry room block, masonry storage features, external hearths, pits, rock alignments, and three human burials. Mapping and spatial data collection at Hokona combined a sub-meter Trimble GeoXT GPS unit and a Nikon DTM-332 Total Station with data collector.

These data were integrated in GIS to create accurate site maps and determine spatial relationships between the cultural materials at the site. A new site datum was established and a 1×1 meter grid was laid out using actual UTM coordinates, allowing for precise horizontal and vertical control. All surface artifacts were pin-flagged, individually point-provenienced, and recorded in UTM space due to the moderately dense artifact scatter. Excavation included shovel-scrape units, midden units, test units, hand units, and trenching strategies, all tied into the grid system. The final mapped site area was expanded from an original survey estimate of 360 m² to 632.9 m² (0.12 acres), based on subsurface features and surface artifact distribution. Total station data were used in conjunction with hand-drawn maps to ensure accuracy and to provide redundancy in field documentation.

PUEBLO I HAMLET/PUEBLO IV AGRICULTURAL SITES | SANTA ANA PUEBLO, NM (2006-2007)

Basemaps:
🟩 Close Up: Thunderforest Outdoors
⚪ Overview: Esri Gray Canvas

From August 2006 to May 2007, I served as crew chief for mapping and excavation at multiple archaeological sites in central New Mexico for the Office of Contract Archeology (University of New Mexico): a late Pueblo agricultural site on Albuquerque’s West Mesa and a large Pueblo I hamlet on Santa Ana Pueblo. I directed total station mapping using a Topcon GTS-226 EDM with a TDS Ranger data collector running SurveyPro 2.0. I established the site datum and excavation grid, mapped all cultural features and architecture, and trained professional archaeologists in total station operation. I managed all spatial data in QGIS to verify geometry, check for locational accuracy, and ensure topological consistency before transferring the files to the project drafter for CAD integration and final reporting.
